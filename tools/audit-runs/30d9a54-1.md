## 3. Findings

### F1. g5-nigeria, checkpoint 1, options[0]
- Old text: (none, new)
- New text: "Workers spend their wages locally, so shops need more staff and tax rises"
- Fault: The wording is ambiguous. "tax rises" usually means a tax increase (higher tax rates). That is a different idea, and it would not create jobs. The key point says the government "collects more tax".
- Evidence: keyPoints[1].content: "The government collects more tax to spend on schools and roads." The feedback for this checkpoint says "More tax pays for schools and roads" and "the government collects more tax". Suggested wording: "...and the government collects more tax".
- Severity: low.

### F2. g5-nigeria, checkpoint 1, options[1] and options[2]
- Old text: (none, new)
- New text: [1] "Factories use machines, so fewer people are needed to make each product"; [2] "The factory sells its goods abroad, so Nigeria earns more from its oil"
- Fault: These are weak distractors. [1] explains fewer jobs, so it does not answer "How can one new factory ... lead to many more jobs?", and a student can rule it out just from the question. [2] is a non sequitur: a Lagos factory's goods (food, drinks, cement, soap) do not earn oil money. Neither would sound right to someone who has not understood the multiplier effect.
- Evidence: keyPoints[1].content lists what Lagos factories make ("food, drinks, cement and soap") and keeps oil separate ("oil gives jobs to few people, because machines do most of the work").
- Severity: low (quality of distractors, not a factual error).

### F3. g5-nigeria, checkpoint 0, options[3]
- Old text: (none, new)
- New text: "Britain ruled it until independence, so it now leads the Commonwealth"
- Fault: The option uses the Commonwealth, which the lesson does not teach until keyPoints[3] ("Nigeria is a member of the Commonwealth"). The checkpoint follows keyPoints[0], so it goes beyond what has been taught up to that key point. The false claim is meant to be wrong, so it is not a factual error in the right answer.
- Evidence: keyPoints[0].content mentions "Britain ruled Nigeria until independence" but not the Commonwealth. keyPoints[3].content: "Nigeria is a member of the Commonwealth."
- Related note (no fault): options[1] ("major oil exporter ... films ... around the world") is a true fact. The lesson files it under global importance ("Globally, it is a major oil exporter..."), so it is correctly wrong for "important in its own region". The wrongFeedback explains this ("Things known around the world show global importance instead.").
- Severity: low.

### F4. g5-nigeria, checkpoint 3, options[0]
- Old text: (none, new)
- New text: "Help paid for through the World Bank or the United Nations"
- Fault: The right answer gives itself away by its form. It is the only option worded as a general definition that repeats the keyTerm wording ("through ... the World Bank or the United Nations"). The other three are concrete examples (UK schooling, a charity's mosquito nets, Chinese loans). A student can pick it by matching words, without understanding. A concrete multilateral example in the same style would test the idea better.
- Evidence: keyPoints[3].keyTerms "Multilateral aid": "Aid given through international organisations such as the World Bank or the United Nations."
- Related note (no fault): options[3], China lending for roads and railways, is taught under Trade, not as aid ("China has also lent money and built roads and railways, though some worry this leaves Nigeria in debt"). It is not multilateral under any reading, so it is correctly wrong.
- Severity: low.

### F5. g5-development, checkpoint 4, options[0]
- Old text: (none, new)
- New text: "Money once paid out as interest can go on health and schools instead"
- Fault: The other three options all start "It ..." (describing what the strategy does). The right answer alone has a different subject ("Money ..."), so its form stands out. This is a mild giveaway. The content is correct and matches keyPoints[4].content ("Money that was paid out as interest can then go on health and schools instead.").
- Severity: low.
