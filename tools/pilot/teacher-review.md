# Electricity pilot: a one-hour review for a physics teacher

Optional (Jason decides). Brief docs/briefs/2026-10-19-evidence-layer.md, step 7.

**What it is.** A revision app for one GCSE student (AQA Physics, combined
tiers). Two lessons on Electricity are being piloted with an "evidence layer": a
quick check before the lessons, a post-test after each, a delayed test a week
later, and predict-first questions aimed at known misconceptions. We would value
a qualified physics teacher's view of the physics and the teaching, in about an
hour.

**Where.** https://jasonmitra-wq.github.io/mabel-gcse/ → Physics → Electricity.
Without an API key the lessons open as slides (no AI). Open "Current, potential
difference and resistance", then "Components — symbols, thermistors and LDRs".
The quick check, the post-tests and "My record" are at the top of the Electricity
page. (Please use a private window: the app keeps its record in the browser.)
The lesson files: lessons/physics/p2-current-voltage.json and
lessons/physics/p2-components.json in https://github.com/jasonmitra-wq/mabel-gcse.

**What to look at (about 60 minutes)**

1. *Physics accuracy* (20 min). Read each key point's full explanation ("Read
   the full explanation"), its "For the exam" box and worked examples. Is
   anything wrong, misleading, or beyond GCSE? Pay attention to: the current
   being the same everywhere in one loop; potential difference as the push and
   as joules per coulomb; ohmic conductors; the filament lamp and diode
   explanations; thermistor and LDR behaviour.
2. *Misconceptions* (15 min). In the widgets, try the "Predict first"
   questions (key points 1, 2, 5 and 7 of the first lesson; key point 1 of the
   second). Each wrong option is meant to match one known misconception
   (list: tools/pilot/misconceptions.md, from IOPSpark and research papers). Are
   the options the ones students really hold? Do the widgets show the point
   clearly, and is anything in the widget's model misleading? (The lamp's
   resistance in the model rises with current, as a stand-in for temperature.)
3. *Exam fit* (15 min). Take the quick check and one post-test. Are the
   questions fair, at the right level, and marked the way an examiner would
   mark them (a separate mark for converting a unit, an answer from her own
   wrong conversion still earning its marks)? The exam evidence is summarised
   in tools/pilot/outcomes.md.
4. *Teaching* (10 min). Would a first-time learner follow it? Is anything
   missing that you would always teach for these specification points, or
   anything you would cut?

**How to reply.** A short list is perfect: where (lesson, key point or
question), what is wrong or could be better, and how you would put it. No need
to write anything up.

**Not in scope.** The AI tutor's conversation (frozen for this pilot), other
topics, and the look of the app.
