- **1.1b** [new, a teacher would correct it] "Second: what is the range of £2, £4, £6 and £8?" — She is told these are maths questions she 'will need', but the range is never used anywhere in the lesson. It delays the real start for nothing.
- **1.3** [F3, a teacher would correct it] "The main money measure is GNI per head" — GNI is never spelt out as gross national income. The word 'economic' is never given for money measures, yet 'social measures' is named at 1.5 and 'economic cause' is used later.
- **1.6** [new, minor] "die before their first birthday, each year" — The 'each year' tacked on the end reads as if babies die each year before their birthday. The wording is muddled for a first definition.
- **1.7** [F8, a teacher would correct it] "Which answer scores both marks?" — The definition was given almost word for word in the message just before (1.6). The right option is also by far the longest, so she can pick it without thinking.
- **1.9** [new, a teacher would correct it] "combines three things into one score between 0 and 1" — She is never told which end is better: that nearer 1 means more developed. Without that the score means nothing to her.
- **1.11** [F8, a teacher would correct it] "Which is a limitation of GNI per head?" — 1.10, the message just before, said 'GNI per head is a mean, so a few very rich people can hide many poor ones'. The answer has just been handed to her.
- **1.11** [new, minor] "It is an average, so it hides how unequally money is shared" — The lesson has said 'mean' up to here (1.1a, 1.3, 1.10) and now switches to 'average' without saying they are the same thing.
- **1.11 wrong answer "It does not show life expectancy"** [new, would confuse or mislead her] "True, but examiners want why it can mislead, not what it leaves out." — Not showing health or quality of life is a perfectly good limitation of GNI per head, and the reply even says 'True'. Marking it wrong on a fine distinction will confuse her; the question has two defensible answers.
- **1.11 don't know (first time)** [new, a teacher would correct it] "Think of the mean of £1, £1, £1 and £97. Does £25 describe most people? - **1.10** [P4, a teacher would correct it] "Every measure has a limitation: a reason it can mislead." — Only the GNI limitation is taught. The key point's other limitations (birth rates and culture, unreliable data, one measure is never enough) are never told, nor is the name 'literacy rate' or the fact that it is a percentage. The key point is closed without them.
- **1.12** [new, minor] "Yes: your line makes every point. You have this: income. You have this: life expectancy. The same pattern recurs at 3.9, 4.9 and 5.10.
- **2.4** [new, minor] "Kenya is one: lower-middle income, still a poorer country." — 'Lower-middle' is used without saying that the middle group is split into upper-middle and lower-middle.

NEE is a different kind of label (Nigeria is both an NEE and middle income), and she is not told this.
- **2.6 wrong answer "NEE"** [new, would confuse or mislead her] "Not quite: Niger's economy is not growing fast through industry. It is an LIC." — Nigeria was named as an NEE one message earlier, so a first-timer will easily read Niger as Nigeria. The reply does not point out that they are two different countries, and it relies on a fact about Niger she was never told.
- **2.6** [F8, minor] "Niger has a GNI per head of about $750. Which group is it in?" — 2.3 has already said 'One of the poorest countries, such as Niger or Malawi, is called an LIC', so the answer was given three messages earlier.

- **2.7** [new, minor] "It fits today badly, because fast-growing countries such as China sit in the "South"." — A line on a map is described with no map to look at, in awkward wording ('fits today badly'), and is never checked with a question. She will not picture it.

- **3.4** [new, a teacher would correct it] "Stage 1: both rates are high, so the population stays small." — High birth and death rates cancel each other, so the population hardly changes. 'Stays small' does not follow from 'both high' and the cancelling is never explained.
- **3.4** [P6, a teacher would correct it] "Stage 1: both rates are high, so the population stays small. - **3.5** [F8, a teacher would correct it] "Predict: in stage 2, what happens to the size of the population?" — The message just before ends 'The population grows fast.' It is not a prediction; the answer has just been told.

## Overall

No, not as it stands. (It is a geography lesson, not physics; I reviewed all six key points on that basis.) The content is mostly accurate, but about seven questions ask for something told in the message just before, so she is rarely made to think. Several things would mislead a first-timer: NEE offered as if it were a World Bank income group, Niger asked about straight after Nigeria, a valid GNI limitation marked wrong, and 3.9 depending on a fact given only in one branch of 3.5. The DTM and the Brandt Line are taught with no graph or map, and key point 5 is a long run of 'Next' messages. Fix those and the machine-sounding own-words feedback and summary lines before she uses it.
