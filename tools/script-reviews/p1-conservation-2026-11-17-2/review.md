## Findings

- **1.1a** [L3, would confuse or mislead her] "First, two quick questions from earlier lessons. Which store does a hot cup of tea have a lot of energy in?" — The lesson opens by testing energy stores (kinetic, thermal, elastic potential) that it has never explained. A first-time learner has not been told what a store is, so she can only guess.
- **1.3** [F3, would confuse or mislead her] "energy moves from its gravitational potential store to its kinetic store" — The gravitational potential store and the kinetic store are used without ever saying what they are (energy because it is raised up; energy because it is moving). The whole ball story rests on them.
- **1.1b** [new, minor] "Which store of the brakes rises?" — Muddled wording: a store does not rise, the energy in it does. The same slip is in the reply 'It is the car's kinetic store that falls'.
- **1.5** [P6, a teacher would correct it] "Energy spread out like that is said to be dissipated. Add the dissipated energy to what the ball still has, and the total never changes." — Three ideas in one bubble: where the energy ends up, the new word 'dissipated', and the total staying constant. The key idea of the law (the total is unchanged) is tucked on the end with no question on it.
- **1.6** [new, a teacher would correct it] "A system that no energy can get into or out of is called a closed system." — A bare definition with no reason given for it, and 'system' itself is not explained. The script never says that the total energy of a closed system stays the same; 1.2 says the law 'holds for everything, every time', so she cannot see why 'closed' matters.
- **1.7** [new, minor] "In the thermal stores of the ball, floor and air" — 1.5 said the energy ends up 'in the thermal store of the surroundings', and now the ball itself is included. The ball is not its own surroundings, and the change is not explained.
- **1.8** [new, minor] "Yes, your line has it: on each bounce some energy is dissipated to the surroundings (heating, sound); and no energy is destroyed." — The praise reads as a pasted mark scheme ('(heating, sound); and'), not a sentence a tutor would say. The prompt is also two questions in 'one line'.
- **2.3** [P8, minor] "A car engine burns fuel to make the car move. Which energy is useful?" — The question stem states the engine's job ('to make the car move'), so the kinetic-store option is given away in the question. Also 'The engine noise' is offered as 'an energy'.
- **2.3 don't know (first time)** [new, minor] "What is the engine for? Which button is the energy that does that job?" — Two questions at once, and the second points her at a button rather than at the physics.
- **2.6 don't know (first time)** [new, a teacher would correct it] "Does that mean more or less friction, and more or less heating?" — Two more-or-less questions at once, shown with the same three buttons, none of which is 'more' or 'less'. The hint does not match what she can press.

- **3.1** [F1, a teacher would correct it] "The spreading out of energy into the surroundings is called dissipation." — Dissipation was already defined in 1.5 ('Energy spread out like that is said to be dissipated') and used in 1.8 and 2.2. It is introduced here again as if new.

There is no question on any of it.
- **3.2** [F1, minor] "Brakes get hot as a car slows down" — The brakes example was already used and explained in 1.1b; it is said again here.
- **3.3** [new, minor] "A flow of electricity through a wire is called a current." — Imprecise: a current is a flow of charge, and 'flow of electricity' is the loose phrase we try to stop pupils using. The bubble also carries three things (the current definition, heating of wires, national cables).

## Overall

No, not as it is. The physics is mostly sound and the tone is kind, but it opens by testing energy stores it never explains and leans on them throughout, so a true first-time learner is guessing from step 2. Several questions are answered by the bubble just before them (4.4, 4.8, 4.14c), some hints and replies do not fit (the skateboard 'rubbing', the more-or-less hints, the pasted mark-scheme replies), and the double-glazing and cavity-wall steps would leave her puzzled about gaps. It is also a long run of Next and three buttons with no picture or hands-on part, so I would want the stores explained, those questions reworked and something to do or see before she uses it.
