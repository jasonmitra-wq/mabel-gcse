## Findings

- **1.1a** [F3, a teacher would correct it] "Where does the energy a machine wastes usually end up? ... "In the thermal store of the surroundings"" — The very first question depends on "thermal store of the surroundings", a phrase this lesson never explains. A first-time learner can only guess, or pick the button that sounds most scientific.
- **1.1a (right answer and every other branch)** [F3, a teacher would correct it] "Yes: it is dissipated into the thermal store of the surroundings." — "Dissipated" is used in every reply here and is never explained anywhere in the lesson. She needs a plain gloss such as "spread out so it can no longer be used".
- **1.5** [P6, minor] "Efficiency is the proportion of the energy supplied that is transferred usefully. The equation is: efficiency = useful output energy transfer ÷ total input energy transfer. For the lamp, 20 ÷ 100 = 0.2." — The definition, the equation and the first calculation all arrive in one bubble. "Proportion" is also a word many 15-year-olds would not follow without "fraction" or "share" beside it.
- **1.9 (end of key point 1)** [P4, minor] "In one line: what is efficiency, and what is the highest it could ever be?" — Key point 1 closes without the electric heater being close to 100% efficient, and without sound as a common form of waste. Both are in the lesson file's key point 1.
- **1.9** [F8, minor] "what is the highest it could ever be?" — The reply to the question just before (1.8) has told her "efficiency is never more than 1 (100%)". Half of this own-words task is therefore copying the previous bubble.
- **2.1** [F3, minor] "It transfers 375 J usefully to a kinetic store." — "Kinetic store" is not explained in this lesson. Something like "375 J goes into making it turn" would do the same job without a new term.
- **2.3** [F5, a teacher would correct it] "Efficiency works with power too. Power is the energy transferred each second, in watts (W). Divide the useful power output by the total power input, just as with energy." — Power and the watt are both introduced in the same bubble as the new use of the equation. The power form is never written out as an equation, and nothing says why dividing powers gives the same answer.
- **2.4** [new, minor] "A lamp is supplied with 10 W, and 9 W of that comes out as light." — The lamp in 1.2 was 20% efficient and now a lamp is 90% efficient, with no word that this is a different kind of lamp. The LED is not introduced until 3.3, so she may wonder which is true.
- **2.5** [new, a teacher would correct it] "Put the numbers into the equation first, then rearrange. For example, 0.4 = useful ÷ 200 gives useful = 0.4 × 200 = 80 J." — The rearranging step is not shown: nothing says "multiply both sides by 200". "Useful" is suddenly shorthand for useful output energy transfer, and the example has no context. This is the hardest step so far and it deserves its own worked example card.
- **2.8** [new, would confuse or mislead her] "A heater is 80% efficient and transfers 400 J usefully. What is the total input energy, in joules?" — She has only been shown how to find the useful energy by multiplying. Finding the total, with the unknown on the bottom of the fraction, has never been shown, so she cannot do this from what she has been told.
- **2.8 don't know (first time)** [new, a teacher would correct it] "Swap the total and the 0.8." — This is a trick with no reason given. A pupil who is stuck will not know what "swap" means here or why it is allowed.
- **2.8** [P16, minor] "A heater is 80% efficient" — The lesson's own key point says an electric heater is close to 100% efficient because heating is its useful output. A heater is a poor choice of device for this question.
- **3.5** [new, a teacher would correct it] "Why is a petrol car engine much less efficient than an electric motor?" — She has never been told that petrol engines are less efficient than electric motors, or anything about engines at all. She can only answer by ruling out two silly options ("destroys some energy", "Petrol has no energy in it"), and the reply never says why the electric motor does better.
- **3.5 ("Petrol has no energy in it")** [F3, minor] "petrol has lots of energy in its chemical store" — "Chemical store" is used without explanation.
- **3.6 don't know (and the marking point)** [P16, a teacher would correct it] "the one with the higher efficiency wastes less energy" — This is not correct as stated. The more efficient device wastes a smaller share of its input, not necessarily less energy; a big efficient motor can waste more joules than a small inefficient one. Step 3.1 had it right with "wastes less of the energy it is given".
- **4.1** [F1, a teacher would correct it] "Lubrication means putting oil between moving parts. It reduces friction, so less energy is wasted by heating" — This is the same fact she was quizzed on and told in 1.1b ("oil reduces friction, so less energy is wasted by heating the parts"). There it was presented as coming from an earlier lesson; here it is taught as new.
- **4.2** [P6, minor] "Insulating hot parts, like a boiler, means less energy escapes to the surroundings. Swapping in better parts, like LEDs for incandescent bulbs, helps too." — Two separate ways of improving efficiency share one bubble. "Insulating" is not explained.
- **4.4** [F8, a teacher would correct it] ""It wastes less energy, so it needs less input, so bills are lower"" — The bubble just before (4.3) has said "needs less energy put in ... lower electricity bills". The right button is simply the longest one and repeats what she has just read.
- **4.4 don't know (first time)** [new, minor] "The best answer links three things: waste, input and bills. Which button links all three?" — The hint lets her find the answer by matching three words to a button. It does not help her think about why an efficient fridge costs less to run.
- **4.4** [new, minor] "An exam asks why a family should buy an efficient fridge. Which answer would score the marks? ... Each link earns a mark." — This is exam-technique talk in a first-meeting lesson. It turns a physics question into a mark-scheme question, and "saves energy scores nothing" is asserted without saying why it is too vague.
- **4.5 (all / none)** [new, a teacher would correct it] "Yes, your line has it: a way: lubrication, insulation or better parts; and less energy is wasted." — The reply reads like the marker's checklist pasted in, not a sentence. "A way: lubrication, insulation or better parts" does not respond to what she actually wrote.
- **4.9** [P16, a teacher would correct it] "a heater has a total power input of 50 W and a useful power output of 20 W" — A heater that is 40% efficient contradicts the lesson's own statement that an electric heater is close to 100% efficient. Use a motor or a lamp.
- **4.12 ("Forgot to multiply by 100")** [new, minor] "Not quite: efficiency over 1 is impossible, so they divided the wrong way round." — The reply does not address her choice. It should say that forgetting to multiply by 100 leaves a decimal below 1, so it cannot give 1.6.
- **whole script** [P14, a teacher would correct it] "Button: Next" — There is no picture, energy-flow diagram or anything to try in the whole lesson: it is text, buttons and typed numbers from start to end. A Sankey-style bar, or a slider splitting input into useful and wasted, would suit this topic, and she may well get bored without one (M1).

## Overall

Not quite as it is. Key point 1 is mostly sound and well paced, apart from the unexplained store vocabulary, but key point 2 asks her to rearrange the equation with almost no teaching: 2.5 skips the step, and 2.8 sets a rearrangement she has never seen, with a "swap" hint that explains nothing. The petrol engine question (3.5) rests on facts she was never given, the heater examples (2.8, 4.9) contradict the lesson's own statement about heaters, and "wastes less energy" in 3.6 is loose physics. I would fix those, sort out the oil fact being both "earlier" and new, and add at least one picture or hands-on step before she uses it.
