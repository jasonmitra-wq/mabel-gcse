- **1.6 wrong answer '1 J'** [new, minor] "Not quite: 500 J. A watt is a joule each second, so 500 W means 500 J every second." — This is the same reply as for a right answer. It does not say why 1 J is wrong: that 1 J each second is 1 W, not 500 W.
- **1.8** [F8, minor] "A heater has a power of 3 kW. What is its power in watts?" — The message before gave '1 kW = 1000 W. A 2 kW kettle has a power of 2000 W', so the answer is modelled immediately before the question.
- **1.9** [P6, a teacher would correct it] "What sets the power? A bigger potential difference gives each bit of charge a bigger push, so more energy. A bigger current sends more charge through each second. Both give more energy each second." — Four ideas are packed into one bubble: a question, a claim about potential difference, a claim about current, and a conclusion. A first-timer needs these one at a time.
- **1.9** [P16, a teacher would correct it] "A bigger potential difference gives each bit of charge a bigger push, so more energy." — This is loose physics. Potential difference is the energy transferred per unit of charge; 'a bigger push, so more energy' leaves out the link, and 'each bit of charge' is vague.
- **1.10** [F8, a teacher would correct it] "Now explain it: In one line: why do you think the lamp got more energy each second?" — Step 1.9 has just told her that a bigger potential difference and a bigger current both give more energy each second. ## Overall

Not quite as it is. The calculations are correct and the worked examples are clear, but I would fix three things before she uses it: the circuit in 1.10 carries a resistor switch and charge readings left over from another lesson and its explanation is given away by 1.9; the reply to 3600 in 2.7 misdiagnoses the commonest mistake; and the marking in 3.8 rejects a correct method the lesson itself showed. Several questions simply repeat the sentence before them with the number changed, so she is copying rather than thinking, and terms such as 'store' and 'work is done' are used unexplained. Key points 2 and 3 also need something to do other than tap Next and multiply two numbers.
