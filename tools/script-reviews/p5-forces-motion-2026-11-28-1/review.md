## Overall

No, not as it is. The physics is mostly sound and the wrong-answer replies are generally well aimed, but several things would mislead a first-time learner: 's' used for distance beside '(s)' for seconds, the 500 m and 53° that come from nowhere, the deceleration working that contradicts the lesson's own 'final minus starting' rule, weight and air resistance used as forces before force is taught, and graphs taught wholly in words with no picture in the script. The terminal-velocity questions and the hammer-and-feather question are answered by the message before them, and the own-words marking calls complete answers 'partly there'. I would fix those and split it into shorter sittings with something hands-on, since six key points in one unbroken run is too much.
